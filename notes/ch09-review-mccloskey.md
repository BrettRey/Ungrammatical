## Chapter 09: Without Grammar (non-grammatical)

### Strengths
The Ethan transcript at the start is a real piece of evidence. CHILDES data, with timestamp, with mother's contributions, in the actual cadence of how a one-year-old talks: a non-linguist will believe what comes next. This is exactly how a trade book should ground its claims about language acquisition.

The Lois Bloom *Mommy sock* example is a classic and earns its place. The Riau Indonesian dialogue ("You go where? / Market.") is the kind of concrete cross-linguistic evidence that does work. The Japanese banana example showing that English doesn't mark a unit/mass distinction in the definite is sharp. The Old English dual pronoun *wit* point ("we two") is a small revelation: English has lost a distinction without trauma.

The structural argument that ungrammatical communication is not just a stage of childhood but persists into adulthood (gestures, emoji, advertising) is a valuable corrective. The chapter is reaching for something useful: the difference between violating an existing grammar and operating without one.

### Major concerns
This chapter has a serious source-grounding catastrophe at lines 177-179. The Hurford quotation is **garbled OCR or scan damage**: "condensing a mess variety, and sections of this chapter so las, creoles pagins, the B normal innate capac sign languages give us a windedles." This appears to be a corrupted PDF extraction that has not been corrected. **A reader will lose all confidence the moment they hit this paragraph.** The chapter quotes Hurford verbatim and the verbatim is gibberish. Cut or replace immediately, with the actual Hurford passage.

The Sumatra travelogue at lines 95-106 is wallpaper. Six paragraphs about volcanic soil, the Malacca Strait, Arab traders, and Chinese influences before the chapter returns to the linguistic point. A trade-book reader can take one paragraph of geographic context. Six is encyclopedia-fill.

Multiple sentences trail off mid-thought: "John McWhorter, who cut his teeth in linguistics working on creoles." (line 122). "But David Gil s" (line 124). "One such language, documented" (line 154). "Now, returning to Riau Indonesian," (line 175). These are scaffolding that hasn't been built.

The Hurford bullet-list at lines 127-134 ("**Creoles:**") is an LLM dump masquerading as exposition. Same content appears in the surrounding prose. Pick one and delete the other.

The McWhorter mention is dropped mid-sentence. The Gil reference at lines 165 (the *gol cantik* quotation) is unintroduced; the reader doesn't know who Gil is or why his analysis matters.

There is also an unhelpful pattern across the chapter of writing the lyrical conclusion before the argument. "It echoes the world of Ethan, where words are free from grammatical constraints, where communication is a dance of sound and meaning." "It's a language that speaks not just to the mind but to the soul, resonating with the very essence of what it means to communicate." These sentences are generated by sentiment and tell the reader nothing.

### Priority fixes
- **Fix the garbled Hurford quotation.** Re-extract from the actual book or remove. This is non-negotiable.
- Cut the Sumatra travelogue to one paragraph (or one sentence: "Riau is on the coast of Sumatra, near the busy Malacca Strait").
- Finish or cut the trailing fragments at lines 122, 124, 154, 175.
- Cut the LLM-style "**Creoles:**" bullet list (lines 127-134); the prose around it covers the same material.
- Cut the lyrical closings ("a dance of sound and meaning"; "to the very essence of what it means to communicate"). These tell the reader nothing.
- Introduce Gil and Hurford properly. Two sentences each. The reader needs to know who's making each claim.

### One concrete suggestion
The chapter has a beautiful three-part structure waiting to be made explicit: ontogeny (Ethan), phylogeny (Riau Indonesian), and logogeny (Hurford on the birth of language). Use that structure. Open with Ethan, end with logogeny. In each section, make one claim: (1) Children communicate without grammar before they have grammar; (2) Some adult languages communicate with much less grammar than English does; (3) Human language probably evolved from communicative meaning before it evolved syntax. Then a closing paragraph that brings the three together: ungrammaticality is not a single thing. There are at least three: pre-grammatical, agrammatical, and contra-grammatical. The book is mostly about the third. This chapter shows that the first two are real and ordinary. That's a payoff.

### Rhetoric that works
The Ethan dialogue. Words and laughter and "uhoh" arranged on the page exactly as the mother and child made them. Nothing the chapter says afterwards lands as well as those twenty lines.

### Rhetoric that doesn't
"It's a language that speaks not just to the mind but to the soul, resonating with the very essence of what it means to communicate." This is church-newsletter prose. Soul, essence, communicate; three abstract nouns in a row, followed by a near-tautology. It's also mistaken: the chapter has just argued that Riau Indonesian is a regular language with less grammatical machinery, not a mystically simpler one. Don't romanticize what your evidence has shown to be ordinary. The argument is more interesting than the lyric.
