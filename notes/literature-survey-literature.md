## Ch 1: The road is long long

The literature folder has very thin direct coverage of reduplication and
contact-feature intensification. The opening will need outside sources for
its empirical core. The relevant pieces that *are* present are about how
"variants" become noticeable in the first place, which is the move ch 1 makes.

- `literature/noticing-identifying-and-discriminating-sociolinguistic-variants-in-england.md` - on how speakers notice and identify sociolinguistic variants; relevant if the long-long opening is framed as a "what counts as a variant?" problem.
- `literature/eckert2008-indexical-field.md` - Eckert's indexical field, useful for any move from "this sounds odd" to "this carries social meaning."
- `literature/Identity in variationist sociolinguistics.md` - Drummond and Schleef on identity in VS; backdrop for the classroom scene.
- No items found on reduplication proper (e.g. African or Asian English doubling, *small small*, *long long*). Will need an outside source (Schneider; Mufwene; Kachru) added to `localbibliography.bib`.

## Ch 2: The asterisk

Coverage of the prescriptive / philological backdrop is partial. The asterisk
itself (Schleicher's *Lautgesetz* convention, Sweet, comparative method) is
not represented; supply externally. What the folder does have:

- `literature/murray1824-english-grammar.md` - Lindley Murray's *English Grammar* (1824); flagship pre-modern prescriptive grammar; useful for setting the prescriptivism backdrop.
- `literature/Haspelmath-Comparativeconceptsdescriptive-2010.md` (= `literature/86.3.haspelmath01.md`, `literature/haspelmath2010.md`) - Haspelmath's history of cross-linguistic comparison from the comparative tradition forward; closest thing in folder to the comparative-method history.
- `literature/pullum2014-fear-and-loathing-of-the-english-passive.md` - Pullum on prescriptivist confusion about the passive; useful as the modern face of the same instinct that the asterisk formalised.
- No items found on Schleicher, Henry Sweet, or the explicit history of the asterisk diacritic. Source these externally (Anttila; Robins; Morpurgo Davies *History of Linguistics IV*).
