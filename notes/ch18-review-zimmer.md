## Chapter 18: Swearing

### Status

This chapter is a stub flagged in the task brief as undeveloped. The current text consists of an unattributed Atwood-sounding epigraph (likely from *The Handmaid's Tale*: "We were the people who were not in the papers..."), a second epigraph apparently from the same source on the impossibility of saying a thing exactly, a posed thesis question (is swearing norm-violating because it's obscene or because it's ungrammatical?), and three paragraphs of LLM-generated reflection on swearing as a "secret language hidden within our own" with "twisted grammar."

### What belongs

A real swearing chapter has a wealth of empirical material to draw on. Here is what the chapter could include:

1. **Timothy Jay's psycholinguistic work.** Jay has been studying swearing for forty years. He has corpora. He has frequencies. He has age-of-acquisition data showing children acquire taboo words by age 1-2 and reach near-adult competency by 11-12. He has neuropsychological evidence (left hemisphere for normal speech, right hemisphere and limbic system for emotional/taboo words - which is why Tourette patients can't suppress coprolalia and aphasic patients sometimes retain swearing when other speech is gone). The aphasia case is exactly the kind of cross-domain anchor the chapter needs.

2. **The grammar of swearing.** *Fucking* as an infix (*abso-fucking-lutely*) is one of English's only true infixes, and the rules for where it can go (always at a stress boundary, *Ala-fucking-bama* but not *Alab-fucking-ama*) are real and grammatical. The McMillan 1980 study and the McCarthy 1982 paper on infixation rules are canonical. This is a paragraph that should be in this chapter.

3. **Cross-linguistic taboo grammar.** What counts as obscene varies wildly. Quebec French uses Catholic liturgical terms (*tabarnak*, *câlice*, *hostie*); standard French uses sexual and excretory terms. Mandarin has a rich set of insults around relatives, especially mothers. Each culture's taboos shape the syntactic possibilities of profanity in that culture.

4. **The "negative polarity" of taboo intensifiers.** *I don't give a damn* / *I don't give a shit* / *I don't give a fuck* but not *I give a damn* (without negation or counterfactual). These are real NPIs, mentioned in chapter 12 in a different context. The connection between taboo intensifiers and NPIs is ready-made.

5. **Steven Pinker's chapter in *The Stuff of Thought*.** Pinker wrote one of the best trade-book treatments of swearing and the chapter could engage with it.

6. **The Tourette evidence.** Coprolalia in Tourette syndrome is an actual neuroscientific case for swearing being processed by a different system than other speech. About 10% of Tourette patients have it. The phenomenon is dramatic and tells us something specific about which brain structures police social grammar.

### One concrete suggestion

Pick one moment of real swearing in a real situation and walk into it. Bono on stage at the 2003 Golden Globes saying "this is really, really fucking brilliant" - which prompted an FCC ruling, a Supreme Court case (*FCC v. Fox*), and a serious legal argument about whether *fucking* used as an intensifier was indecent because it was sexual or because it was just a swear word. That's the chapter in nuce: when is swearing ungrammatical and when is it just illegal? When does a word become a trespass, and when does it become a structure? Bono and the FCC make the question physical. Then bring in Jay's data, the infixation grammar, the Tourette evidence, and Quebec's *tabarnak*. A chapter is there, waiting to be written.
