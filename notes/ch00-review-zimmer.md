## Chapter 00: Introduction

### Strengths

The opening hook works. The asterisk as guiding symbol, then the six glorious oddballs from CGEL ("He offered her the steak fiendishly hungry"), gives the reader an instant sensory grip on the problem. Schleicher dying of tuberculosis at 47 with unfinished projects, Sweet's preface quote about *it is me*, the asterisk's prior life as a textual-criticism mark for repeated lines and Origenic gaps - this is exactly the kind of provenance trail that makes a symbol feel like an artifact rather than a convention. The Indonesian harbour memory at the start of the personal section is the most vivid passage in the chapter: small rocky islands, perfectly calm dark sea, sun just up. That's the prose to keep. The Kinokuniya floor with the Cambridge Applied Linguistics red covers and the legs hurting - same. Tachiyomi as a noun is a gift to the reader.

### Major concerns

The chapter has two distinct registers: the lived-memory register (Indonesia, Tokyo, Humber) and an LLM-padded register that sounds nothing like the same writer. The Schleicher section drifts toward biographical filler: "his approach was not merely academic," "an unshakable belief in language as a natural phenomenon," "obsessive desire for clarity." These are placeholder adjectives standing where a scene should be. We never see Schleicher actually working. Where is the room? The dictionaries? The cold hands? The frustration over a Sanskrit form that won't fit?

The Sweet treatment fares better but still lacks a body. We're told he went to Heidelberg and that "his passion for language was ignited" - tell us what he heard there, what he saw on a German street, what the lecture room sounded like.

The Jeremy Taylor segment runs aground twice. The line "his library was confiscated" begs to land somewhere, but vanishes into "Despite these hardships." A confiscated library is a *scene*. And the closing paragraphs collapse into two AI tics: "is at the heart of the modern linguistic understanding" and "central to any serious study or use of language." Both should go.

The "missing" tooth-to-*dent* reconstruction is the chapter's central illustration of what the asterisk does, and it's done telegraphically. Walk us through one cognate set with the actual sound shifts, on a single word, slow.

### Priority fixes

1. Cut the two summary paragraphs that end the Taylor section ("This insight is at the heart..." through "central to any serious study or use of language"). They add nothing the surrounding prose hasn't said.
2. Restore a sense of physical Schleicher - one anecdote, one room, one tactile detail. The "philosophy proved unsatisfying" abstraction is the giveaway it was generated.
3. The unattributed line "great folly to declaim against us" from Taylor needs page numbers or at least a verified citation; check this against the source rather than trust prior drafting.
4. The bigeneric ungrammatical glide sentence "even you wanted to, you couldn't" needs to be set up. Right now it appears mid-sentence as if from another draft.

### One concrete suggestion

For the missing-word reconstruction passage, walk the reader through one tooth: in Schleicher's hand, on a piece of paper with German *Zahn*, Dutch *tand*, Gothic *tunþus*, Latin *dens*, Greek *odontos*, Sanskrit *danta* arrayed in front of him. Show him crossing out alternatives, settling on the *d-* and *-nt-* skeleton, and writing *\*dent* with the asterisk. That's the moment the symbol is born. The current text gestures at it. A Zimmer-style scene would let the reader watch it happen.
