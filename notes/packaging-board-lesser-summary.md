# Packaging board: Wendy Lesser

## Cross-chapter prose-level patterns

This is a book with two voices in the same body. The Reynolds voice is plain, observational, and intellectually honest. It tells real stories about real people: Schleicher repurposing Aristophanes of Byzantium's marker, Hankamer and Postal getting "whose" wrong, Mr. Boyko catching identical bubble-sort code, Doctorow emailing back to confirm "even it's never approved" was intentional, Steven Coats hunting double modals through a billion-word YouTube corpus, John Payne noticing in correspondence that "much" is an NPI in some constructions and not others. When this voice is on the page the book is a contender for the trade list of any university press.

The other voice is the LLM-explainer voice that has crept in during prior assisted drafting. Its symptoms are predictable: "It is important to note that...," "stands as a poetic affirmation," "speaks not just to the mind but to the soul," "weaves a complex tapestry," "in the grand scheme of things," "this vast, unfolding story," "the never-ending dance of language." It produces five-bullet "implications" lists and three-bullet "challenges" lists. It writes summary paragraphs that summarize summaries. It generates parallel passes through the same material (Chapter 4 has three overlapping treatments of the same seven ethical frameworks; Chapter 5 has the constructionist subsection reproduced verbatim). And it leaves chat artifacts in the manuscript: "Certainly. I'll build out the two examples..."; "You're right. I apologize..."; "% copied from 01.tex"; raw ChatGPT output on emotion-as-vector-space at the end of Chapter 5.

The lean chapters are 7, 10, 12, 14, 19, and 21. Each is built around a central observation, a real anecdote, and one or two figures or examples. They read like Reynolds. The overwritten chapters are 4, 5, and 15 (each two or three times their right length). The stub chapters are 6, 8, 11, 18, 20, and parts of 16. Chapters 9, 13, and 17 sit in the middle: real material framed by AI-explainer prose.

## Where the book is consistently lean vs consistently overwritten

Lean: the diachronic case studies. The much/very story, the probable-to-play story, the passé composé story, the "long long" reduplication story, the do-support history. These chapters know what they are: small archival victories in a frame that connects them.

Overwritten: the theoretical chapters. Chapters that try to lay out a framework (the Parfit-on-ethics conceit in 4, the Barrett-on-emotion plus Bayesian-predictive-processing plus neuroscientific-perspective in 5, the seven-ethical-frameworks survey, the unified theory) bloat under formulaic structure: "Approach X says Y, here's an example, here are some implications, here are some challenges, in conclusion..."

## What one editorial repair would do the most work

Cut roughly thirty percent. Not by trimming around edges but by deleting whole subsections. Specifically: in Chapter 4, delete two of three duplicate framework surveys; in Chapter 5, delete the duplicated subsection 4.5, the second Proust quote, the literary-epigraph cascade, the standalone Barrett section, the standalone neuroscientific-perspective section, and the ChatGPT vector-space dump; in Chapter 15, delete the second-pass whose investigation, the world-tour-of-whose section, and the five-item "lessons" list at the end. Throughout, hunt down and delete every "let's pull this all together," "as we'll see," "you might be wondering," and "in the grand scheme of things." Remove the visible LLM artifacts ("Certainly...," "You're right...," "% copied from").

What remains, when those cuts are made, is closer to the book Reynolds is actually trying to write: a trade-style argument for a homeostatic-property-cluster account of grammaticality, illustrated through a series of specific, well-archived cases. The bones are there. The decoration is what's smothering them.
