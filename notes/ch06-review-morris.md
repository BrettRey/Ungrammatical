## Chapter 06: Prescriptivism on the right and on the left

### Strengths

There is one good idea here. The German contrast: Anglophone feminists fight to neutralize gendered nouns; German feminists fight to make them visible. Susan Neiman noticed it. The chapter notices it. It's a real reversal of expectation, the kind of fact that makes a reader sit up. The book's spine is "what people mean by ungrammatical depends on what they're using the word to do," and this is one of the cleaner illustrations of that argument so far.

The Geoffrey Nunberg quotation is also doing real work: "racists don't use slurs because they're derogative; slurs are derogative because they're the words that racists use." That is a sentence that travels. It carries the chapter's broader argument in eighteen words.

The discussion of disability rights organizations naming themselves The Canadian Federation of the Blind, with the position that "blindness is not a handicap, but a characteristic," is the kind of specific institutional move that has more weight than the general philosophical argument around it.

### Major concerns

This chapter is a stub. Forty lines. It opens with five citations packed into the first three paragraphs (Cameron 1998, Lakoff 1973, Miller 1976, Spender 1980, Baron 1986, Maggio 1998), all from the 1970s and 1980s, all from a single thread of feminist-language scholarship. That is the chapter performing its bona fides while saying very little. The Diewald 2018 and Vervecken 2012 citations on the German case are the only ones doing actual evidentiary work.

The chapter ends with what looks like an LLM exchange about Beaver and Stanley's "Good Vibrations" (Henry Schiller). Lines 30-41 are clearly copy-pasted from a chat. "You propose an interplay of formal constraints\dots" is an LLM addressing Reynolds in second person. "Citing them might enrich your argument that\dots" is LLM advice. None of this should be in the manuscript. It's reviewer's notes left lying on the desk.

Section 25, "Metphorical extensions" (typo in the heading), is the only place in this chapter where a real argument starts to develop, and it gets one paragraph. The Jones 2017 quote ("Blindness should never be equated with darkness") is the kind of specific bit of language-policing that should be the chapter's center. Reynolds also has a smart move here: paraphrasing Nunberg to apply the same logic to woke vocabulary policing. Both ends of the political spectrum are doing a similar pattern of word-meaning ownership. That's the chapter. It needs a body.

On the protagonists question: who is the prescriptivist on the right? Nobody named. Who is the prescriptivist on the left? Nobody named. Susan Neiman is named but does not become a person. Cameron and Lakoff and Spender are footnote material. The chapter title promises a comparison between two political pieties, but no specific human being is shown enacting either piety. The reader doesn't know whether to feel sympathy or scorn for the people Reynolds is calling out. The argument is currently floating above the people who would carry it.

This is exactly the kind of chapter where Errol Morris would want a face. One real teacher correcting one real student. One real diversity training session. One real *Daily Mail* editorial. One Twitter pile-on. Not as anecdote, but as the chapter's grounding event.

### One concrete suggestion

Open with one specific, named, dated incident from each side. A copyeditor at the *Atlantic* refusing to print *Latinx*. A grandmother at Thanksgiving correcting a teenager's *they*. The Society for Plain English finding *waitress* sexist; the Verein Deutsche Sprache fighting Gendersternchen. Two real moments of language-policing, one from each political pole, set side by side. Then ask: what do these scoldings have in common? What does the word *ungrammatical* (or its everyday cousin *wrong*) do for both sides? That comparison, with two real people in two specific rooms on two specific days, is the chapter the title promises. The current version has the title and nothing under it.
