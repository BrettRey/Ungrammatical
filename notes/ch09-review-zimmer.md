## Chapter 09: Without Grammar

### Strengths

The opening transcript with Ethan, 11 months and 21 days old, and his mother bouncing the ball - "Yeah it's your ball. Bounce, bounce, bounce. Ready? Bounce, bounce, bounce, bounce whoops!" - is exactly the kind of human ground-level material the chapter needs. *Ball. Baby. That.* Three single-word utterances and a giggle. A reader can hear the toddler. The anchored-in-time precision (down to the day, and the URL pointer to the actual TalkBank file in a comment) is the right kind of seriousness for a science book.

The Lois Bloom *Mommy sock* example is well chosen: same two words, two different meanings, no syntax to disambiguate.

David Gil on Riau Indonesian is potentially this chapter's centerpiece, and the final block quotation about *gol cantik* (`beautiful goal' / `the goal is beautiful') being genuinely vague between predicate and attribute is fascinating and concrete. The point that English grammar forces a choice that Riau leaves underspecified is the kind of cross-linguistic scene that makes a reader think about their own language differently.

The Yagua footnote (seven tenses) is a small gift.

The Japanese banana sentence, *banana tabeta*, with its interpretations as "I ate a banana / the banana / did you eat the bananas" carries the same point efficiently.

### Major concerns

The chapter has visible stitching damage. There's a paragraph (line 178) that has been corrupted in the OCR or the conversion - "condensing a mess variety, and sections of this chapter so las, creoles pagins, the B normal innate capac sign languages give us a windedles. or humans with on the ambient hasity for language so..." This is a destroyed quotation. It must be restored from the source.

The Riau geography passage is generic travel-guide prose: "lush vegetation," "tapestry of cultural influences," "melting pots of diverse cultures." That whole section can be cut. A reader doesn't need an encyclopedia article on Sumatra; they need to know where Riau Indonesian is spoken and roughly by whom.

The Person A / Person B "you go where? / market" Riau dialogue is a paraphrase, not data. We need actual Riau, glossed, with the gloss showing what's missing. As written, it could be a constructed example for a textbook on simplified-English speech, not a real cross-linguistic anchor.

Two named speakers fall off the page mid-sentence: "John McWhorter, who cut his teeth in linguistics working on creoles." (full stop) and "But David Gil s" (full stop). Both need completing.

The "Pragmatic Communication / Creative Language Use / Conclusion" mini-section reads like an essay outline filled in by an LLM ("Whether through simplicity, creativity, or technical precision, ungrammatical communication transcends the boundaries of traditional language structure, reflecting the multifaceted nature of human expression"). Cut.

The Hurford "two-stage development" summary is paraphrase-of-paraphrase. We're told meaning developed first, then grammar, three times in three paragraphs.

### Priority fixes

1. Restore the corrupted Hurford/Givón quotation. Without it, the chapter ends in word salad.
2. Cut the Sumatra travel paragraphs entirely. They are placeholder material.
3. Get a real Riau Indonesian conversation, glossed properly. The Gil 2014 chapter has them; reuse one or two exactly as published.
4. The Hurford summary needs to do less and the data needs to do more. Two paragraphs of Riau-makes-no-predicate-attribute-distinction outdo any number of "language preceded grammar" assertions.
5. Finish the truncated McWhorter and Gil sentences.

### One concrete suggestion

Find a moment of David Gil at work in Riau. He has been there for years; he has fieldnotes; he has stories. The chapter mentions him only as an authority. A trade reader needs to see him in a conversation - a Riau speaker shrugging at a sentence that English would force into either *the goal was beautiful* or *a beautiful goal*, telling Gil it's just *something to do with a goal and beauty*. That moment is the chapter's argument made flesh. Pursue it. If Gil hasn't published the anecdote, write him; he is reachable. If you cannot get a scene from his work, then a moment from your own attempt to learn Italian (already in the chapter) where you registered an absent grammatical distinction would also do, scaled smaller.
