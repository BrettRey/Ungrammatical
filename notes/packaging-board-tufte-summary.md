# Tufte review summary: cross-chapter visual rhetoric

The book is making a sophisticated multi-domain argument about grammaticality and is doing so almost entirely in prose. Across 22 chapters there are 17 figures, of which I count perhaps four or five that genuinely earn their space: the *very*/*much* frequency curve (ch12), the impossible-languages gradient (ch17), the dependency-distance arcs and trash-out gradient (ch19), and the cow split-panel (ch1, qualified). Most of the remaining figures are decorative, AI-generated stock illustrations (ch1 cow, ch9 burggeat, ch21 black birds, ch12 generic sigmoid, ch17 angry lightbulbs) or single-purpose photos that don't carry analytical load (ch2 Archaeopteryx, ch15 vowels-without-units, ch19 cluttered OpenStreetMap screenshot).

## Where the book is consistently well-illustrated

The book uses **typographic small-multiples** beautifully: numbered example sets where parallel sentences are aligned by starring (ch2's dog-fetching gradient, ch3's seven inversion pairs, ch9's child-language transcripts, ch15's mother-son context contrast, ch19's seven-step trash-out gradient, ch21's three-language reduplication paradigm). When the book is showing rather than telling, it shows with starred-asterisk-aligned typographic data, and this is real Tufteian work.

## Where the book consistently relies on prose for things visuals could do

Pretty much everywhere else.

1. **Comparison tables.** Ch4 spends 22 pages comparing seven ethical frames against several test sentences. That is a 7x6 matrix, not 22 pages of prose. Ch6's English-vs-German left-vs-right paradox is a 2x2 grid. Ch9's typological gradient is a multi-column compatibility grid. Ch13's Englishes-Venn would be better as a directed graph of asymmetric judgement.
2. **Historical timelines.** Ch5's *whose* spans 1325 to 2024. Ch12's Jespersen Cycle has three stages across 800 years. Ch20's relative-pronoun history needs one. None has a timeline.
3. **Quantitative curves.** Ch10 quotes attestation counts of *November the* across decades but never plots them. Ch12 has the actual *much*-NPI percentages from Brett's own corpus work, plotted nowhere; instead a generic decoration sigmoid stands in. Ch3 asserts passive frequency claims that beg for a small chart.
4. **Structural diagrams.** Ch7 argues for hierarchical discrete combination and never draws a tree to show it. Ch14's *enshittification* derivation is a six-brick stack delivered as eight paragraphs. Ch12's three-stage Jespersen cycle is described in prose. Ch11 (neurolinguistics) has no brain figure.
5. **The model of grammaticality itself** (introduced in ch1's lines 304-396 as a 14-item nested numbered list, never rendered as a diagram) is the book's central scaffolding and would benefit most from a single visual reference. Right now every chapter that wants to call back to "the model" has to retell it.

## Recurring authorial patterns

Repeated across many chapters: scaffolding fragments left in (chs 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 16); duplicated sections (ch4 has three rounds of the same seven ethical frames; ch5 has duplicated subsections; ch15 reduplicates ch5's *whose* analysis); decorative AI-art that adds no analytical content; and LLM-summary closings ("In the grand scheme of things," "the never-ending dance of language") in ch4 and ch12.

## The single visual repair that would do the most work

A **canonical "model-of-grammaticality" diagram** built once, in chapter 1, and referenced throughout. A horizontal axis from "fully grammatical" through "context-coerced" through "satiable rare types" through "architecturally blocked." Above the axis, vertical drop lines mark example types. Below the axis, three coloured horizontal bands show where reanalysis can reach (semantic, social, structural motivations). Each example sentence the book discusses gets a single tick mark on this axis with a tiny label.

Building this once, in ch1, would let:
- Ch2's gradient examples (the seven dog-fetching sentences) plot directly onto it.
- Ch4's seven ethical frames plot as different verdict-shadings on the same axis.
- Ch10's spray/load and *probable to play* mark specific zones.
- Ch12's *much*-NPI shift plot as a moving tick mark across centuries.
- Ch15's *whose*, *Rubymar*, and *go-yesterday* plot as boundary cases.
- Ch17's impossible-languages gradient meet the natural-language end of the axis.
- Ch21's reduplication facts plot as predictable structural-block items.

Right now the book has a single thesis distributed across 22 chapters of overlapping prose. One shared diagram would unify the argument, expose the duplications (ch4, ch5+ch15+ch20), and give the reader a single mental map for the entire book. That is the highest-leverage visual repair, and it costs about one good page of artwork to build.
