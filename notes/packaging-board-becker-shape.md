## Overall shape: Becker's take

### What the book is currently doing structurally

The book opens with the asterisk, drifts into Brett's biography, and then spends roughly six chapters wandering through a kind of catalog: islands (01), gradience (02), conventions (03), morality (04), feelings (05), politics (06). Only at chapter 07 does generative grammar get its own treatment, and only at chapter 10 does the title concept (real patterns) appear. The middle (11-19) is a grab bag: neurolinguistics stub, *do*-support and *much*/*very*, dialects, fashion, codeswitching, impossible languages, swearing stub, efficiency. Chapter 20 is two-thirds-empty, and chapter 21 (the *long, long way* finale) returns to a single concrete construction and walks the reader through it with Old English, Russian, Japanese, Jamaican Creole. The HPC framework, which the project description tells me is the spine, is barely visible in the table of contents and never named there. It surfaces fitfully in places like 02's "Architectural Constraints" passage, but it does not organize the sequence.

### What's working at the level of shape

The asterisk-as-overture is genuinely good. Schleicher to Sweet to Householder is a clean three-beat opener that introduces the symbol, the shift from reconstruction to ungrammaticality, and the early acknowledgement of variation. A reader knows what the book is about by page three, even if they don't yet know the argument.

Chapter 21 is the right kind of ending. *A long, long road* / *the road is long, long* gives a single small puzzle that escapes monolingual English, picks up through Japanese, Russian, Jamaican Creole and North-East Ambae, and resolves in the historical accident of Old English compound nouns. That is the move the book has been promising. It earns the title.

The cross-domain ambition is also working in principle. A reader who finishes the book will have seen "ungrammatical" applied to syntax, child language, dialect, register, fashion, codeswitching, impossible languages and efficiency; that's the demonstration the cover promises.

### What's not working at the level of shape

The audience question is unsettled, and the structure pays for it. Chapter 02 spends fifty lines on phrase-structure rules and then drops a numbered taxonomy of grammaticality conditions that reads like an appendix from a paper. Chapter 04 has consequentialist/deontological/contractualist subsections labeled like a philosophy survey. Chapter 19 walks the reader through dependency-distance arithmetic. Meanwhile chapters 14 (fashion) and 16 (codeswitching) read like trade-book essays. The book is alternately addressing linguists who will recognize the moves and a curious lay reader who will not, and the pacing assumes both.

A reader doesn't know what argument the book is making until quite late. The introduction announces "what grammar really is and how we acquire it"; chapter 01 reframes it as "how do we distinguish between what's grammatical and what is not?"; chapter 02 ends with "grammaticality is an emergent property"; chapter 03 reframes again around conventions. By chapter 06 a reader still doesn't know whether the thesis is "grammaticality is gradient", "grammaticality is conventional", "grammaticality is moral", or "grammaticality has many sources." Each chapter does some of the work, but the cumulative effect is one of accretion rather than argument. The HPC payoff (this is one homeostatic kind that gathers many causal properties) is the move that would tie the diversity together, and it is neither named in the TOC nor staged at any obvious turning point.

The middle is where readers fall off. A generative grammarian opening this TOC sees: islands, gradience, *probable*-vs-*likely*, intuitions, then four chapters on morality / politics / generative grammar / coordination interleaved, then fashion, codeswitching, swearing, efficiency. The "fashion" chapter heading alone is an invitation to put the book down. Not because the chapter is weak (it isn't, on a fast read) but because the order signals "this is a book that uses linguistic ungrammaticality as an entry point to social commentary," which is precisely the framing that lets the tribe ignore it.

The structure also leaks solidarity in the other direction. Chapter 07 is titled "The Generation Gap" and uses Chomsky-as-villain framing. Chapter 06 carries political baggage on its sleeve. Chapter 04's heavy ethical-frameworks section is a long detour for any reader who doesn't already share the descriptivist priors. These are the moves that signal "I'm with you" to one camp at the cost of being heard by the other. A linguist who isn't already on the descriptivist-functionalist team has been told, by the table of contents, that this book isn't for them.

Three chapters are stubs (11 neurolinguistics, 18 swearing, 20 getting-grammaticality-wrong) and at least two more (06 politics, 07 generative, 16 codeswitching, 17 impossible) are notes-and-quotes. That's not a shape problem yet; it's a state-of-completion problem. But the shape decisions have to be made before those stubs are filled, or you'll spend writing effort on chapters that the structure doesn't need.

### What would change the shape for the better

The book wants three acts, not twenty-one wandering chapters.

**Act I: the asterisk and the puzzle.** Keep 00 and 01 intact. Move 02 (gradience) here as the demonstration that the asterisk is not a yes/no. Cut the toy generative grammar from 02 and merge it with the relevant parts of 07. Drop the numbered taxonomy. End Act I with the explicit claim: people use "ungrammatical" to mean many different things, and the book will trace the diversity to a single homeostatic kind.

**Act II: the diversity.** This is where the cross-domain catalog goes, but it needs ordering. The cleanest spine is "grammaticality and what tracks it." Sequence: 09 (without grammar / child + Riau) -> 10 (real patterns) -> 12 (becoming ungrammatical, with *much*/*very* and *do*-support combined) -> 03 (being grammatical isn't enough: convention) -> 13 (across language boundaries: dialect) -> 16 (codeswitching) -> 14 (fashion: innovation as glamour) -> 04 (morality, heavily trimmed) -> 06 (politics, heavily trimmed). The ethical-frameworks survey in 04 should go; what survives is the AAVE habitual *be* discussion plus a short taxonomy.

**Act III: the theory and the limits.** 05 (intuitions / how grammar feels) belongs here, not earlier; it explains the detector after the reader has seen what it's detecting. Then 19 (efficiency) and 17 (impossible languages) as the question of what the system can and can't do. Then 07 + 08 as a single chapter on what the generative tradition got right and what it missed. Then 11 (neurolinguistics) if it can be drafted. Then 21 as the case-study finale.

Concrete moves by chapter number:

- Reorder so the sequence runs roughly: 00, 01, 02, 09, 10, 12, 03, 13, 16, 14, 04, 06, 05, 19, 17, 07+08, 11, 21.
- **Combine** 07 + 08 into a single chapter on the generative legacy. Right now they overlap.
- **Combine** 12 + 20 (the *whose* material in 20 is part of the becoming-ungrammatical story).
- **Cut** 18 (swearing) unless it can be promoted to a section inside 14 (fashion) or 13 (across language boundaries). As a standalone stub it will not earn its keep.
- **Split** 05 (intuitions) is too long at 900 lines; it's doing work for two chapters.
- **Retitle** 14 to something less consumer-magazine. "Innovation as glamour" or "When the ungrammatical becomes a style" is the actual argument.
- **Add** an explicit Act II opening that names the HPC frame in a sentence a non-philosopher can follow. Without it, the diversity reads like a list, not a thesis.

### The one structural move

Name the HPC argument in the introduction and put it in the table of contents. Right now the book performs the diversity and trusts the reader to assemble the theory out of it. That's the wrong order for a trade book. State the claim early ("what people mean by *ungrammatical* is a cluster of related but distinct judgments held together by overlapping causal properties, not a single underlying rule"), label the three acts on the contents page, and let chapter 21 close the loop by showing the cluster operating on a single construction. Once that spine is visible, the reordering above becomes obvious; without it, the chapters will keep drifting back into a catalog, and a generative reader will keep closing the book at the fashion chapter.
